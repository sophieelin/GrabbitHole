## Bad Situations

### 1. Picking research back up means rebuilding it from memory

When conducting research, it's easy to forget much of what you've already researched after a pause. For example, after a week of midterms, Joe comes back to the project. While he can look at his search history or a long list of sources, it's hard for him to remember what mattered the most, how they connected, or what he planned to look into next. He spends a ton of wasted time reopening pages and rereading them just to figure out where he left off. Moreover, many of the connections between sources and potential ideas are stored only in his head, so even after a day or two, much of that can be forgotten and lost, and he might have completely forgot why he stored a link as important or read later.

### 2. Re-exploring ground that's already been covered

When group members research separately, they often end up reading the same sources or going down paths a teammate has already explored and ruled out. The same thing can happen to individuals, and people revisit pages they've already read without realizing, and time gets wasted covering the same ground twice.

### 3. Research is hard to summarize for teammates

Within a group, it's hard to quickly verbally describe research findings and list out sources and their connections. The only effective way to share research is to write a long summary by hand, and many don't have time for that just for a quick check-in.
