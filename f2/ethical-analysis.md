# Ethical Analysis

## 1. Privacy vs collaboration
**Dimension:** Values  
**Prompt:** Value Tensions 
**Feature:** Automatic browser tracking
**Consequence:** Automatically recording browsing activity makes research easier to share, but may expose personal searches or unrelated websites. This creates a tension between collaboration and privacy.
**Design response:** Allow users to start and stop tracking manually, and review which pages are shared with their group.

## 2. Unintended use of research history
**Dimension:** Stakeholders  
**Prompt:** Non-targeted Use  
**Feature:** Shared research graphs and contribution histories
**Consequence:** Although Grabbithole is designed for collaborative research, someone could use browsing histories to monitor teammates' activity or judge how much work they contributed. This could create unnecessary pressure and discourage exploration.
**Design response:** Avoid displaying productivity metrics based on browsing activity, such as time spent researching or number of pages visited.

## 3. Changing research habits
**Dimension:** Time  
**Prompt:** Adaptation  
**Feature:** Research graph visualization
**Consequence:** Over time, users may begin prioritizing research paths that produce cleaner graphs rather than following their curiosity. They might also become more selective about what they explore because they know their activity is being recorded.
**Design response:** Allow users to explore privately and choose which discoveries to preserve in their research graphs.

## 4. Unequal control over contributions
**Dimension:** Values  
**Prompt:** Value Tensions  
**Feature:** Users can suggest new sources or connections to another member's research graph, but the graph's owner decides whether to accept or reject them
**Consequence:** Giving the graph's owner final approval protects their control over their research, but it also means other members have less say in what gets included. Useful contributions might be rejected simply because the owner disagrees with them.
**Design response:** Allow graph owners to provide reasons for rejecting contributions and let contributors continue developing their ideas in separate research graphs.

## 5. Widespread sharing of research
**Dimension:** Pervasiveness  
**Prompt:** Widespread Use 
**Feature:** Sharing research graphs and connections between sources
**Consequence:** If Grabbithole becomes widely used, misleading or poorly supported connections could spread across multiple research groups. Users might assume that connected sources support the same conclusion even when the relationship is uncertain.
**Design response:** Visually distinguish automatically tracked navigation from user-created connections, and allow researchers to add explanations or correct relationships.
