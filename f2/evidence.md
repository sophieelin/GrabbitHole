## Evidence

### 1. Picking research back up means rebuilding it from memory

- **Research gets interrupted constantly.** In an observation study of information workers, 57% of their units of work were interrupted before they were finished ([Mark, Gonzalez and Harris, CHI 2005](https://www.interruptions.net/literature/Mark-CHI05-p321-mark.pdf)).
- **People are afraid of losing their place.** A CMU study found that people keep browser tabs open as reminders, or because they fear having to search for the information again ([Chang et al., CHI 2021](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/522203)).
- **People couldn't recall why they saved a link** For most users in the "Keeping Found Things Found" study, bookmarks gave neither a reminder nor any context for why a page was relevant. Users said bookmarks often weren't descriptive enough to recognize ([Jones, Bruce and Dumais](https://typeset.io/pdf/keeping-found-things-found-on-the-web-xc410w5ysk.pdf)).
- **Saved links rarely get used.** When 50 participants tried to get back to pages they had bookmarked, they used their bookmarks to find only 16% of them ([Bergman et al., 2021](https://bespacific.com/?p=80082)).

### 2. Re-exploring ground that's already been covered

- **People redo searches all the time.** A year-long study of Yahoo search logs found that as many as 40% of all queries were attempts to re-find something the person had already found ([Teevan et al., SIGIR 2007](https://people.csail.mit.edu/teevan/work/publications/papers/sigir07.pdf)).
- **People forget they already saved something useful.** Researchers observed that people often don't remember having saved useful material at the moment it would actually help ([Marshall, Communications of the ACM](https://cacm.acm.org/?p=2201)).
- **Group research duplicates effort.** A common group strategy is for each person to research on their own and compare notes later, which easily leads to duplicated work ([Morris, CHI 2008](https://archive-irsg.bcs.org/informer/?p=171)).

### 3. Research is hard to summarize for teammates

- **Groups don't share what only one member knows.** A well-replicated line of research shows that groups spend more time discussing information everyone already knows than information only one member has ([Stasser and Titus, 1985](https://atlas.northwestern.edu/papers/information.pdf)).
- **Talking it through isn't enough.** Even when group members were willing to share everything, they couldn't remember enough of what they knew to get the full picture across ([Lightle, Kagel and Arkes](https://www.asc.ohio-state.edu/kagel.4/HP_Manuscript_MS_11_26_08.pdf)).
- **A visual map makes someone's research easy to follow.** When people built on someone else's research map, the earlier person's thought process was easy to see, and the map's organization helped more than its content ([Fisher, Counts and Kittur, CHI 2012](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/distributed-sensemaking-improving-sensemaking-leveraging-efforts-previous-users/)).
- **Structured summaries save the next person time.** When earlier research was saved in a structured form, the next person understood it about three times faster than people working from the raw web pages ([Liu et al., UIST 2019](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10152057)).
