## Solution Sketch: Grabbithole

Grabbithole will act as a tool for visualizing and saving research paths. While working on a research project, researchers can use Grabbithole's browser extension to record the websites they visit during a research session. These links will be added to a "research graph" on Grabbithole's website, with the option to include notes explaining the importance and relevance of each added link. Users can also edit the connections between these links to mirror their discovery process. The research graph will be displayed visually as a set of nodes and lines, with the nodes representing links and the lines representing connections.

Collaborative projects will involve multiple researchers working on the same shared research graph. Each researcher individually uses Grabbithole's browser extension, but their findings can be proposed as contributions to the shared graph. For example, two researchers working on a project about the history of glass might both come across an article about Roman glassblowing techniques. One researcher then follows a link about Roman architecture, while the other follows a separate link about Roman paint colors. Once their contributions are accepted, this will be displayed on Grabbithole as one node representing the Roman glassblowing article, with one connection leading to Roman architecture and another leading to paint colors. Metadata and other visual cues will indicate the respective authors of each contribution.

Researchers can also fork existing graphs to explore different directions without modifying the original. For example, a researcher may look at someone else's graph and find a link that interests them but leads them down a different research rabbit hole. They can fork from that node and begin recording their own research process in a separate graph. Grabbithole will record which graph the fork originated from and where the researcher branched off. Researchers may also propose changes to another graph through contribution requests, which give the graph owner or authorized collaborators the option to review and approve additions or deletions before they are incorporated.

Other users can also search through Grabbithole and treat it as an archive of research paths. If their search matches a node, the research graph containing that node can be displayed, even if the graph originally explored a different topic. Users can then follow the research paths to discover other sources, fork the graph to continue their own research, or propose additions to the existing graph if contributions are enabled.
