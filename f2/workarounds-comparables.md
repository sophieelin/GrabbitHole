# Workarounds and Comparables

## Workarounds

### Saving Sources for Later

Researchers often use browser bookmarks, Google Docs, Notion, or open tabs to save interesting articles and sources for later. These tools are familiar and convenient, allowing researchers to quickly save resources without interrupting their browsing.

However, bookmarks typically store links without preserving why a source was interesting or what the researcher intended to investigate further. Open tabs can also become difficult to manage as collections grow, cluttering the browser and risking the loss of saved resources if the browser closes or the computer shuts down. Grabbithole allows researchers to save sources directly from their browser and attach personal notes and context to each link, making it easier to remember why they saved something and what they wanted to explore next.

### Retracing Previous Research Paths

Researchers often rely on browser history, bookmarks, or personal notes to revisit previously explored sources. Browser history provides a chronological record of visited pages, while bookmarks and notes help researchers keep track of resources they found useful.

However, these tools do not preserve the relationships or sequence of discoveries that led from one source to another. Researchers must manually reconstruct how they arrived at a particular finding, making it difficult to pick up where they left off or avoid repeating previous exploration. Grabbithole visualizes the paths researchers take as they browse, allowing them to retrace previous explorations, revisit earlier branches, and continue investigating from where they stopped.

### Sharing Research and Its Context

Research groups commonly share interesting findings through Slack, Discord, group chats, or shared Google Docs. These tools make it easy to distribute links and communicate findings, while written notes can provide additional context about why a particular source is relevant.

However, shared links often show only the final finding rather than the intermediate sources and exploration that led to it. Although contributors can write detailed explanations, collaborators are unlikely to read through everyone's notes or reconstruct each person's research process. As a result, group members may unknowingly repeat one another's work or miss connections between discoveries. Grabbithole makes individual research paths visible within a shared graph, allowing collaborators to see not only what others found but also how they arrived there. Notes attached directly to sources and connections provide additional context without requiring members to read through lengthy explanations.

## Comparables

### Raindrop.io

[Raindrop.io](https://raindrop.io/) allows users to save webpages, organize bookmarks into collections, add tags, and annotate saved resources. It provides a convenient way to collect and retrieve sources without relying on open tabs or browser history.

However, its organizational structure primarily centers on collections and tags rather than the paths through which researchers discover resources. It does not inherently capture how one source led to another or how individual discoveries connect within a larger investigation. Grabbithole instead emphasizes preserving these discovery relationships and allowing researchers to explore them as interconnected paths.

### Notion

[Notion](https://www.notion.com/) provides shared workspaces where research groups can organize links, write notes, and collaborate on documents and databases. Its flexible structure makes it useful for maintaining centralized research repositories and documenting findings.

However, organizing resources and documenting their relationships requires manual effort. Researchers must actively create pages, update databases, and explain connections between sources. This can interrupt the discovery process and makes it difficult to preserve the actual sequence of exploration. Grabbithole aims to capture these connections as researchers browse rather than requiring them to reconstruct their research process afterward.

###  ResearchRabbit

[ResearchRabbit](https://www.researchrabbit.ai/) helps researchers discover related academic papers through interactive citation networks and recommendations. Users can start with an existing paper and explore related works, identify connections between authors and publications, and share their findings with collaborators.

However, its discovery process primarily focuses on academic literature and citation relationships rather than the researcher's broader browsing journey. While it helps users discover related papers, it is less suited to capturing arbitrary connections between different types of online resources or preserving user-directed exploration across them. Grabbithole focuses on researchers' own discovery paths, allowing them to decide where their curiosity takes them and enabling others to follow, revisit, branch off from, and contribute to those paths.  

Additionally, while ResearchRabbit allows users to share collections, its core experience is centered on individual paper discovery rather than collaborative research exploration. Grabbithole focuses on group discovery, allowing researchers to share their browsing paths, see how others arrived at particular sources, and build on one another's explorations. Rather than relying on automated recommendations to determine what users should explore next, Grabbithole preserves the connections researchers make themselves.
